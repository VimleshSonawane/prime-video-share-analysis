# What drives Prime Video's share of US TV time?

A portfolio analytics project: a repeatable Python pipeline and a four-view Tableau dashboard that look at how streaming has reshaped US TV viewing and what moves Prime Video's share of it.

**Dashboard:** [Tableau Public link — add after publishing]

## The business question

Streaming's share of US TV time nearly doubled in five years, from 26% (May 2021) to 49% (July 2026). Inside that shift, what actually moves Prime Video's share, and what does that imply for how the business operates?

## Key findings

1. **Every new Prime Video high since September 2023 came in a live sports month.** Successive records: 3.6% (Sept 2023), 4.0% (Dec 2024), 4.3% (Dec 2025), 4.5% (May 2026), each coinciding with Thursday Night Football or the NBA playoffs.
2. **Game nights nearly double share.** On Thursday Night Football nights in October 2025, Prime Video reached 6.4% of TV versus a 3.8% monthly average (Nielsen).
3. **The baseline is growing too.** In comparable non-sports months, Prime Video rose from 3.2% (June 2023) to 4.2% (June 2026), so sports drive the peaks but not all of the growth.
4. **A title's value is concentrated right after launch.** In Netflix's H1 2026 engagement data, titles released that half-year were 2.8% of the catalog but 18.4% of viewing (6.6x their weight). Titles from 2025 drew 1.6x; older titles under 1x.

## Data sources

| Source | What it provides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nielsen, *The Gauge* (monthly, 2021–2026) | Share of US TV time by category (streaming, cable, broadcast) and by platform (Prime Video, Netflix, YouTube) | Compiled by hand from monthly releases into `raw/gauge_tracker.csv`; every row has a source link and a source tier |
| Netflix, *What We Watched* (Jan–Jun 2026) | Hours viewed, views, release date and global availability for ~17,400 titles | Official engagement report, `raw/netflix_engagement_2026H1.xlsx` |
| Event timeline | Prime Video live sports and major launches | `raw/prime_video_events.csv`, each event sourced |

## How the pipeline works

`pipeline.py` runs in three stages so a new data drop can be re-run end to end:

1. **Quality checks**: duplicate detection, category shares capped at 100%, share of figures from Nielsen's own releases vs trade press, methodology breaks counted, and the Netflix file reconciled to Netflix's published total (97.7B vs 97B+, pass).
2. **Cleaning**: tags each Prime Video month by live sports context, joins the event timeline, buckets Netflix titles by release recency.
3. **Output**: four Tableau-ready CSVs in `output/` plus `output/data_quality_report.txt`.

### Run it

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 pipeline.py
```

## Data quality notes

- **Methodology breaks are excluded from trends.** In February 2023 Nielsen moved live TV watched over the internet (vMVPDs) out of streaming, so January 2023 is not comparable with later months. February 2026 carries a methodology dispute. Both are flagged in the data and excluded from trend lines.
- **Source tiers.** About a quarter of Gauge figures are confirmed against Nielsen's own releases; the rest come from trade press reporting Nielsen figures. Where two sources conflicted (for example January 2026), the value matching two sources was kept and the conflict noted.
- **Gaps.** The Gauge series is a verified sample of months, not every month. Charts show dots for reported months; lines connect across unreported months.
- **Undated Netflix titles** (mostly licensed) are kept as their own bucket; dropping them would discard half of all viewing.

## Limitations and next steps

- Correlation, not causation: sports months coincide with Prime Video's highs, but the Gauge can't isolate sports from other launches in the same month.
- Prime Video's NBA regular-season months (from fall 2025) are not yet tagged; only confirmed events are.
- Fill the remaining monthly gaps from Nielsen's archive and upgrade secondary-source rows to Nielsen's own releases.
- Netflix moves from half-year to annual engagement reports in 2027, which reduces the cadence of title-level data.
